Water Treatment Process

Overview

For APSC 101, our team (TastyWaterEng Inc.) was tasked with designing and building a small-scale water filtration system for a hypothetical community. The system had to use coagulation, flocculation, and filtration to reduce the turbidity of contaminated water by at least 500 NTU in under 8 minutes, while staying within tight constraints on 3D-print time, print volume, material usage, and maintenance level.

Each team member owned a specific component of the prototype: the press plate, mixing impeller, gearbox, coupler, dowel stabilizer, and threaded nut. The design centred on two main subassemblies. A mixing subassembly used a motor-driven dowel with embedded magnets to magnetically couple to — and spin — an impeller inside the coagulation tank, driving the fast-mix, slow-mix, and settling stages needed to form flocs. A press-plate subassembly used a gearbox to reduce a DC motor's speed while preserving torque, turning a threaded rod that raised and lowered a filter press plate to push the treated water through a coffee-filter membrane.

To hit the print-volume and material budget, parts were filleted and hollowed wherever possible: the press plate and gearbox housing used cutouts, the threaded nut enclosure was hollowed out, the impeller was reduced from five fins to three, and the dowel stabilizer was redesigned in a cross shape instead of a straight bar to keep the same rigidity with less material. An Arduino-based control system automated pump timing, motor speeds, and turbidity sensor readings, and a dedicated emergency-stop button could immediately cut power to every motor and pump.

Across repeated trials, the prototype consistently completed the full treatment cycle in about 5 minutes (well under the 8-minute target) and reduced turbidity by 500–2000 NTU, exceeding the 500 NTU requirement. Sensors and pumps performed reliably run after run, though the 3D-printed gearbox and impeller components showed wear after heavy repeated testing — the main improvement identified for a future iteration is moving those high-cycle parts to a more durable material.
